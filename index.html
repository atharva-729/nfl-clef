Can an AI model watch an NFL broadcast and tell us what is happening, frame by frame?

We gave Cloudflare's Clef one still frame every 5 seconds from the 4th quarter of Jets at Bengals (Week 8, 2025) and asked it the same 24 multiple-choice questions about each one: the score, the clock, who has the ball, what kind of shot it is. We then turned its answers into game events (touchdowns, conversions, reviews, the lead change) and checked everything against the official play-by-play. The aim: see whether this is accurate and cheap enough to drive auto-clipping, live app updates and archive tagging.

What this means

  • Every score was caught: all 4 touchdowns, both extra points and both two-point conversions, on the frame the scorebug changed.
  • The story of the comeback is there: the tie at 2:00, the lead change at 1:54, and the turnover on downs that sealed it.
  • The overturned two-point call at 7:52 was read correctly: review shown, then the score moving to 32.
  • No false alarms. Nothing was reported that did not happen.

The 3 misses

  • Review at 10:24: on screen for one frame; the detector waits for two. With a one-frame setting it is caught (15 of 17, still no false alarms).
  • Bengals timeout: Clef is unsure counting the small timeout dashes.
  • Game end: our rule looked for a kneel-down, which Clef never called.

The short version

  • Reading what is printed on screen is essentially solved. Both scores, the clock, the down and the scorebug message were right on every frame where the scorebug was visible (316 frames).
  • It reads the scorebug equally well on any shot: game action, coach close-ups and sideline shots all scored 98%.
  • Judgement questions are much weaker. Highlight-worthy, safe-for-an-ad-break, replay and yards-to-goal barely beat, or fall below, the always-guess line.
  • Two scorebug weak spots: counting the Bengals' timeouts (85%), and possession when the Jets have the ball (11 misses).

Over half of all answers come with 90%+ confidence, and those are right 99% of the time. Low-confidence answers can simply be ignored, which is what the event detector does.

How it was done

Frames. 408 stills, one every 5 s, covering the whole 4th quarter (about 34 minutes of video).
Questions. 24 fixed multiple-choice questions, plus a short description of where the scorebug is and what to ignore.
Clef. Each frame goes in with a zoomed crop of the scorebug. Clef returns a probability for every option, in about 2 seconds.
Truth. Scorebug labels read from every frame and cross-checked against the official play-by-play. Other labels drafted by AI and spot-checked.
Events. An answer must be 60%+ confident and hold for 2 frames before it counts. Recorded once; this page only replays files.

Caveats

  • We made it easy. We told Clef exactly where the scorebug is, what each part means and what to ignore, and gave it a zoomed crop. A different network's graphics would need a new description.
  • One quarter, one game, one broadcast. Some answers barely vary here (quarter, Jets timeouts), so their scores prove little.
  • The labels were read by one AI reader from the same frames. The play-by-play agrees on all but 12 frames, but nobody independently re-labelled.
  • Stills, not video. Anything that needs motion or the previous few seconds is out of reach.
  • Event timing is measured against the scorebug, not the live play. The scorebug itself updates some seconds after the play.

The asterisks *

  • Nine questions carry soft labels: replay, play phase, yard markers, yards to goal, celebration, highlight-worthy, ad-break safe and the two sponsor questions.
  • Their labels are one reader's judgement from a small still, only spot-checked, and known to contain mistakes.
  • Sponsor labels are the weakest. Small logos, such as a headset brand, were missed in the labels, so Clef looks worse here than it probably is.
  • These numbers are indicative only and are kept out of the headline 98%.

What we left out

  • Clef only ever saw stills. One frame every 5 s; none are stored in the repo.
  • No YouTube player. The video above streams from a copy of the 4th quarter hosted for this demo with permission, so the answers can be drawn on it.
  • Questions a still cannot answer were dropped: referee signals, and "has the whistle blown".
  • Not attempted: the game clock to the second, player identification, or the play type (run or pass).
  • The smaller, cheaper Clef-Flash model has not been run on this footage.

What comes next

  • Add the commentary. Give Clef the last 10 seconds of captions with each frame. The scorebug questions are already near-perfect, so the value is in new questions the picture alone cannot answer: what kind of play it was, who was involved, why a flag was thrown.
  • Make it harder. Fewer hints about the layout, a different broadcast or network, and a full game, to see how much of the 98% survives.
  • Fix the known gaps cheaply: read "TIMEOUT" and "FINAL" from the scorebug message, and react to a single frame for reviews.
  • Get the judgement labels reviewed by a person before quoting any highlight, ad-break or sponsor figure to a client.
